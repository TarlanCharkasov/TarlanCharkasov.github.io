What I do

Four things, done properly.

Most work falls into one of these. If yours sits between two of them, say so — the scope is usually easy to shape once I understand what decision the numbers need to support.

01

IFRS management accounts

Monthly or quarterly reporting packs prepared to IFRS, with commentary that explains what moved and why — not just a data dump.

  • P&L, balance sheet and cash flow
  • Variance analysis against budget
  • Board- and investor-ready formatting
  • Built-in reconciliation checks
02

Budgets and forecasts

Annual budgets, rolling forecasts and three-statement models with clean assumption logic you can change without breaking anything.

  • Three-statement forecasting models
  • Scenario switches and sensitivity
  • 13-week rolling cash flow forecasts
  • Capital investment appraisal (NPV, IRR)
03

Reporting automation

If you rebuild the same report by hand every month, it can almost always be automated. Excel and VBA, structured properly.

  • Excel and VBA automation
  • Messy spreadsheet clean-up
  • Control totals and audit trails
  • Documented source and handover
04

Dashboards and KPIs

Power BI dashboards built on a proper data model, showing the handful of numbers that actually change decisions.

  • Star-schema data models and DAX
  • Margin, mix and working capital KPIs
  • Power BI or self-contained HTML
  • Walkthrough call on handover

Background

Audit discipline, in-house judgement.

I'm a finance professional based in Budapest, currently working in financial planning and controlling for a consumer-health multinational, where I lead budgeting, forecasting and month-end close for the CEE region and partner with commercial teams on the numbers behind their decisions.

Before that I spent three years in audit at KPMG, ending as a senior auditor across multiple industries. That's where the habit of tying things out comes from — when your job is to check whether numbers hold up, you learn to build them so they do.

The other half of the work is automation. I've built ERP-to-Power BI bridge files and SAP macros that turned multi-day reporting cycles into something that runs in minutes. Most manual reporting is automatable; it usually just hasn't had anyone's attention.

I take on a limited number of freelance engagements alongside my role, which means I'm selective about scope — but it also means the work gets proper attention rather than being squeezed between twelve other jobs.
